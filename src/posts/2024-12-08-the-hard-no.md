---
title: "The Hard No That Made My Stomach Drop"
date: 2024-12-08
pageDescription: "A flat refusal can be clean and final on paper and still cost something in the body."
---
A former manager once asked me to join an advisory board. One meeting a month, he said, plus occasional email threads, which in my experience meant constant email threads.

I liked this person. I respected the product. But I was already stretched past what I could sustain. So I said no, flat and clear: "I can't do this, and I don't think that's going to change in the foreseeable future."

He was gracious about it. That part went fine. What surprised me was my own stomach, which dropped the moment I hit send, even though I already knew the answer was right.

That is the strange thing about a hard no. It can be clean and final on the page and still cost something in the body. Hard no's are like hitting send on an email you cannot take back. The dread lasts longer than the reply does, and then it passes, leaving only the time you protected.
