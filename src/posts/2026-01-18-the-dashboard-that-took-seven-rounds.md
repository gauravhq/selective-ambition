---
title: "The Dashboard That Took Seven Rounds"
date: 2026-01-18
pageDescription: "A simple dashboard took seven rounds of fixes to finally look simple."
---
I wanted something simple. A dashboard that pulled numbers from a spreadsheet and laid them out cleanly. I described it, and the first version looked close. Then I noticed it was pulling from the wrong columns.

I pointed that out. The next version fixed the columns and broke the formatting. I fixed the formatting, and only later noticed it had quietly dropped a filter I had asked for at the very start. I only caught it because a number looked wrong. I added the filter back. The charts then started showing running totals instead of daily numbers, a problem that had not existed two rounds earlier.

I lost count somewhere around round seven. Call it ninety minutes, probably more if I am honest.

The story I told people afterward was clean: I built a dashboard with AI in an afternoon. True, technically. But it left out the part where I spent that afternoon as a quality inspector on a production line, checking the work of something that sounds equally confident whether it is right or completely wrong. That is a specific kind of tired. Not the tired of building something. The tired of catching what almost got past you.
