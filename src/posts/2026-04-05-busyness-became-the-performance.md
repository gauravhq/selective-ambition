---
title: "Busyness Became the Performance"
date: 2026-04-05
pageDescription: "Being busy became proof that you matter, more than whether the work itself meant anything."
---
Someone posted on LinkedIn before five in the morning. "While you were sleeping, I was grinding," it said. Thousands of people liked it.

A coworker mentioned at lunch that she had not taken a vacation in two years. She said it like a badge, almost bragging. Nobody at the table said that sounds terrible. Nobody asked if she was okay. Everyone said some version of "yeah, same."

This is the water we swim in. This is Tuesday.

Somewhere along the way, being busy became proof that you matter. Not the quality of your work. Not whether any of it meant something. Just the fullness itself, full calendar, full inbox, full plate. Say the words "not much, pretty relaxed" out loud and it can feel like admitting you are irrelevant, like you owe someone an apology for having free time.

None of this happened because any one person decided it should. It built up slowly, post by post, badge by badge, until busy stopped being a description and became the whole performance.

Stepping out of that performance on purpose, choosing what actually matters instead of what looks impressive, is the way out. It is a quieter exit than it sounds.
