---
title: "Borrowed Desires in Someone Else's Handwriting"
date: 2025-04-13
pageDescription: "Some of your strongest wants were never yours, they were envy wearing the costume of ambition."
---
I can trace some of my own wants to the exact moment they showed up.

There was a LinkedIn post from someone I had worked with a couple of jobs back. He had built a small tool that automated a reporting task, and the post picked up hundreds of likes, comments calling him a genius. I read it over breakfast on a Tuesday. By that afternoon, "build an automation tool" was sitting on my project list, even though I had no problem that needed automating. I wanted the likes. I wanted people calling me a genius too.

There was also a dinner with a friend fresh out of an MBA program, talking about "strategic thinking" like it was a language I had never learned. Within a week I was researching programs of my own. The gap was never really about education. It was about envy, and envy does not announce itself. It shows up dressed as ambition, sits quietly on your list, and waits for you to mistake it for something you chose.

I call these borrowed desires now. Once you can name one, you start noticing how many of the others are wearing the same disguise.
