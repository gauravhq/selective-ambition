---
title: The Case Against It Is Ancient
date: 2025-06-01
pageDescription: "The idea of having it all is old, and so is the argument against it."
---
The myth of having it all did not start with a magazine cover in the 1980s. That is just when it picked up a catchy name and a wide audience. The idea underneath it is far older than that.

The Stoics argued against it roughly two thousand years before anyone had a buffet to overload a plate at. Marcus Aurelius wrote, privately, to himself, in the kind of notebook nobody expected the rest of us to ever read: "You could leave life right now. Let that determine what you do and say and think."

That is not a productivity tip. It is a filter. If you might not get another day after this one, most of the pile you are chasing stops looking urgent, and a much smaller list of things quietly rises to the top and stays there instead.

The philosophers were not writing about inboxes or notifications, obviously. They were writing about the same basic human tendency to want everything at once and never quite land anywhere solid. The desire to have it all is ancient. So, it turns out, is the case against it.
