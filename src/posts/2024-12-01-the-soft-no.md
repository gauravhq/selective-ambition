---
title: "The Soft No That Revealed the Truth"
date: 2024-12-01
pageDescription: "A soft no meant to buy time ended up revealing what was never really wanted."
---
A colleague once asked me to co-author a technical white paper. It was an interesting topic, good for my profile, but the timing was wrong.

So I gave her a soft no: "I can't take this on right now, but if you're still working on it in the spring, I'd be interested." She seemed to accept that, and for a while I thought it was settled.

Spring came. She had already finished the white paper with someone else. And what I felt, reading her update, was not disappointment. It was relief, clean and immediate.

That surprised me more than the paper itself ever had. The soft no had not only bought me a few months of room. It had bought me enough distance to find out that I never actually wanted to write the thing. I had only wanted to avoid saying no to her, in that moment, in that conversation.

Sometimes a soft no is not a delay tactic. It is a way of discovering what you would have chosen anyway, once the pressure of being asked directly is no longer sitting on you.
