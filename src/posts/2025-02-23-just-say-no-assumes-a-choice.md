---
title: "Just Say No Assumes You Have a Choice"
date: 2025-02-23
pageDescription: "Advice to just say no quietly assumes you have a choice, and many people do not."
---
Advice like "do less" and "choose only what matters" has a quiet assumption buried inside it: that you have a choice. A lot of people do not, not fully, not yet.

Rent is due on the first of the month, every month, no exceptions negotiated. Your kid needs braces and the insurance covers most of it but not all of it, and the gap is still your problem. Your car payment does not care how much you want a simpler life. Your manager assigns the work, and you do not get to pick from it like a menu, because that is not how employment works for most people. Your industry runs layoffs every quarter, and some of your energy goes, quietly, to not being on the next list.

"Just say no" sounds wise on a Sunday afternoon with coffee in hand. It sounds like a fantasy on a Tuesday morning when your performance review is next week and your boss just asked if you can take on one more thing.

None of this means the advice is worthless. It means it is incomplete until it accounts for the person who genuinely cannot afford to say it out loud.
