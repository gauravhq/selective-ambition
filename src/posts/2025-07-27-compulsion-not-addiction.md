---
title: Compulsion, Not Addiction
date: 2025-07-27
pageDescription: "A careful line between a real addiction and a habit I could have broken."
---
I want to be careful with this word, because it matters. For a while my pattern looked like the stories I had heard about addiction. Up at five to check whether a task set running the night before had finished. Queuing more tasks to keep the machine busy during a gym session, timed in advance so nothing sat idle for long. Meals eaten with the screen open in front of me, always on, always pulling gently at the edge of my attention.

But this was not addiction. Addiction is clinical, specific, and it carries a weight I have no right to borrow from people actually living with it. What I had was something smaller and more ordinary. Compulsion.

Here is the difference that matters most. A person with a real addiction cannot simply close the laptop and walk to the kitchen. I could. Nothing was stopping me except my own unwillingness to stop. I just did not want to, which is a very different sentence than I could not.

That gap between could not and did not want to is everything. It is also, in its own quiet way, the beginning of a way out of it.
