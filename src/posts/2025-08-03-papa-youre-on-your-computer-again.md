---
title: "Papa, You're on Your Computer Again"
date: 2025-08-03
pageDescription: "My daughter noticed something about my laptop habit that I could not see."
---
My daughter walked in while I sat at the dining table, laptop open, dinner going cold in front of me. She stood there a second and said, "Papa, you're on your computer again."

I started to correct her. "No, baby, it's not work, it's just," and then the sentence stopped, mid air, because I did not know how to finish it. A hobby? It did not feel like one anymore. Fun? It had stopped being fun weeks earlier and I had not noticed. A project? Which one. I had eleven running at once.

I closed the laptop instead of answering. She climbed onto the chair beside me and just watched, chin on the table, feet swinging underneath her without quite reaching the floor.

I asked if she had eaten. She told me everything, in that thorough way children describe food, every item, every opinion offered freely. I listened. My hands wanted the keyboard. I kept them flat on the table instead.

She did not need an explanation from me. She needed a father who was actually in the room with her, and for those few minutes, that is exactly what she got.
