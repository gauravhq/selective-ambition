---
title: "The Colleague Who Got Promoted"
date: 2025-10-19
pageDescription: "A colleague's promotion can trigger alarm in your body before your mind agrees to it."
---
It was an ordinary Tuesday afternoon, the kind where nothing is supposed to happen and you are just watching the clock crawl toward five. Then a company wide message. A colleague's name, his new title, a line about his contributions.

The first thing I felt was heat behind my sternum. Not anger exactly, something closer to alarm, like a smoke detector going off in a room where there is no fire.

I told myself I was happy for him, and part of me was. He had worked hard. He deserved it. But another part of me spent that evening on the couch, replaying every decision I had made over the past year, looking for the moment I went wrong.

It changed the relationship too, in a way I did not expect. Not dislike, nothing that clean or that dramatic. Just a new awkwardness, as if he had moved to a different country and we were now talking across a border that had not existed the week before. He had not changed. Only his title had. That was enough. Comparison does this quietly, every time. It turns colleagues into competitors without asking either of them, and turns friends into benchmarks.
