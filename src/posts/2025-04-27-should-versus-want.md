---
title: "Should Versus Want"
date: 2025-04-27
pageDescription: "The list of things you should want is long, the list you actually want is short."
---
Ask most professionals what they should do and the list writes itself. Learn a new skill. Start a side project. Get better at public speaking. Read more. Network more. Build a personal brand. Update the portfolio. Twenty items, easy, in under a minute.

Now ask the same person to list three things they actually want. Not should. Want. Watch the pen slow down.

The second list is always shorter, and always harder to make, and that gap tells you something worth sitting with. Somewhere along the way, what you want got buried under what you think you are supposed to want, layer after layer, until the two started to look identical.

They are not identical. A should carries a faint hum of obligation, a sense of catching up with people you did not choose to race. A want has a weight of its own, no explaining required.

The should list will always be longer. That is not a personal failing, it is just what happens when everyone around you is handing you their opinions about your life. The work is learning to hear the shorter list underneath it.
