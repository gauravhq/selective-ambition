---
title: "I Could, But I'm Choosing Not To"
date: 2025-05-11
pageDescription: "A short honest sentence turned out harder to say than any excuse ever was."
---
Here is the sentence I have been practising, badly, ever since. Not "I can't." Not "I don't have time," which usually is not even true. Not some excuse dressed up to look like a constraint outside my control.

Just the plain truth. "I could do this. But I'm choosing not to."

The first time I said it out loud, I was declining a colleague's invitation to join a working group. I heard the words leave my mouth and felt an actual jolt of fear, like I had broken something that could not be repaired afterward.

The colleague just said, "Fair enough," and went and asked someone else. That was the entire response. No argument, no disappointed pause, no follow up message asking me to reconsider the decision.

The sky did not fall. Nothing broke. I simply had an evening back that I would not have had otherwise, and nobody thought less of me for it, as far as I could tell from the outside.

I think it might be the single most powerful sentence in the whole book, precisely because it costs you something small to say out loud and gives you back something large in return.
