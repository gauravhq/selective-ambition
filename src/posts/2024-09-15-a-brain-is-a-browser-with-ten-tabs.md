---
title: "A Brain Is a Browser With Ten Tabs"
date: 2024-09-15
pageDescription: "Working on one thing lets your mind keep solving it quietly in the background, where ten things cannot."
---
When you are working on one thing, your brain keeps working on it even when you are not looking directly at it.

You are having dinner, and an idea for the project shows up uninvited. You are in the shower, and a solution to yesterday's problem arrives out of nowhere. You are walking somewhere unrelated, and your brain is quietly connecting dots in the background.

That does not happen the same way when you have ten things going at once.

With ten things open, your brain behaves like a browser with ten tabs running. Each one is using a little processing power. None of them are getting your full attention, and none of them are getting the quiet background time that actually produces the good ideas.

The shower thought and the dinner-table idea are not luck. They are what focused attention looks like when it has room to keep working after you have stopped consciously trying. Fill the background with too many open tabs, and that process simply cannot fire, no matter how hard you concentrate in the foreground.

One open tab is not a limitation. It is what lets the rest of your mind finally get to work.
