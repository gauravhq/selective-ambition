---
title: "The Hidden Tax of Switching"
date: 2023-03-19
pageDescription: "Switching tasks all day costs you far more focus than it feels like in the moment."
---
Every time you switch tasks, your brain pays a toll to disengage from one thing and re-engage with the next. You rarely notice the toll itself. You just notice, later, that the day felt busy and produced almost nothing.

The researcher Gloria Mark and her colleagues found that it takes an average of about twenty five minutes to return to an original task after an interruption. That delay is not about focus taking that long to rebuild. It happens because once you are pulled away, you tend to get swept into other tasks before returning.

Now multiply that. Switch between email, a chat tool, a tracker, and a dashboard fifteen times in a morning, and the cost is not fifteen small interruptions. It compounds, because each half finished task stays quietly open in your head.

What you lose is not just time. It is depth, the kind of sustained attention that only shows up after you have stayed with one thing long enough to stop being distracted by it.

I counted my own switches one day out of curiosity. Seventeen before lunch. I had touched almost everything on my list and finished none of it.
