---
title: "Not Now, Nine Times"
date: 2024-09-29
pageDescription: "Choosing one project took only a moment, but writing off the other nine took much longer to accept."
---
Once I had picked the one project I actually wanted to build, I went through the other nine on my list and wrote two words next to each of them. Not now.

Not never. Not cancelled. Not a bad idea. Just not now.

It took about three minutes to write nine short phrases. It felt like it took a year.

That gap is the part nobody warns you about. Choosing the one thing was not the hard part, once I was honest about which one it was. Letting go of the other nine was the hard part. Every single "not now" felt like a small death, a version of the future I was quietly closing a door on, even though the door was not locked and nothing was technically gone.

I think that is why so many of us end up with ten things half-started instead of one thing finished. Choosing is fast. It is the small grief afterward, times nine, that we would rather avoid by never choosing at all.

Writing "not now" does not remove the ache. It just gives the ache somewhere to land, on paper, instead of in the back of your mind indefinitely.
