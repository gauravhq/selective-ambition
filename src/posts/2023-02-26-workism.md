title: "Workism"

pageDescription: "For many people work has quietly become a source of identity rather than just income."

Some ideas explain a fact about you specifically. This one explained a fact about almost everyone I know.

The writer Derek Thompson coined the term workism in The Atlantic, to describe how work has quietly become a kind of religion for a lot of modern professionals. It stopped being only a way to pay the bills. It became the place where you find meaning, identity, community, and a sense of your own worth, all stacked into one activity.

Once work is carrying that much weight, slowing down is not a small scheduling adjustment. It can read as an existential threat, because pulling back from work can feel like pulling back from meaning and belonging.

That is why the advice to just do less so often bounces off. It is not a time management problem, and more calendar tricks will not fix it. It is an identity problem, and identity problems need something closer to grief work than to a productivity hack.

Of every idea like this, this was the one that hit closest to home for me. I recognized myself in it immediately, and I did not particularly enjoy what I saw.
