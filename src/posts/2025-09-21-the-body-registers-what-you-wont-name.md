---
title: "The Body Registers What You Won't Name"
date: 2025-09-21
pageDescription: "A trauma researcher's insight about the body storing what the mind avoids, applied smaller."
---
I think about a line from The Body Keeps the Score, Bessel van der Kolk's book on how the body stores what the mind refuses to process. His subject was trauma, a scale I am not about to compare this to. Nothing here is that serious, and I want to be careful about the difference.

But the underlying principle seems to translate down, smaller and quieter. Your body registers what your brain refuses to name. The eye twitch that comes and goes for a week and points to nothing you can identify. The knot between your shoulder blades that stretching never quite reaches. The stomach that tightens when you open your laptop on a Monday morning, not because anything specific is wrong, just because something is always happening and you are never sure which thing to deal with first.

Your brain can refuse to name a feeling. It can call stress by some other name, or by no name at all. Your body does not get that option. It just keeps the record, in the jaw, in the shoulders, in the places you stopped checking a long time ago without noticing you had stopped.
