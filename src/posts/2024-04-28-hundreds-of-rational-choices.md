---
title: Hundreds of Rational Choices
date: 2024-04-28
pageDescription: "Isolation rarely comes from one dramatic choice but from hundreds of small reasonable ones added up."
---
Here is how the drift actually works. It is not dramatic. There is no single moment where you sit down and choose isolation on purpose. There are just hundreds of moments where you choose convenience instead, and isolation is simply what is left over once you add all of those moments up.

My mother calls on Sundays. There were weeks where I let it ring, told myself "I will call her back," and then called three days later instead of that same afternoon. A colleague used to knock on my door around noon and ask "Lunch?" They stopped knocking sometime in week three. I did not notice until week eight.

Each of those individual choices was rational. Defensible, even, if you asked me to explain any single one of them on its own. Letting one call ring is nothing. Skipping one lunch is nothing.

None of those choices felt like they mattered at the time. All of them did. That is the part nobody warns you about, and it is the part worth sitting with before you decide your own small choices do not add up to anything.
