---
title: "Priya and the Search Function"
date: 2025-12-14
pageDescription: "One quiet colleague fixed the one broken thing everyone else was too busy to notice."
---
I watched someone do this once, not in a book, at work. The team was brainstorming new features for an internal tool, and the ideas were flying. A notification system. A mobile version. Integrations with Slack, with Teams, with email, with the calendar. Everyone was saying yes to everything, energy climbing with each new suggestion. Priya sat quiet through most of it, writing nothing down.

When the team lead finally asked what she thought, she said, "I think the search function is broken and that's the only thing users have complained about. Can we fix that first?" The room paused for a second. Then someone said something like "yeah, but what about," and the idea storm picked back up around her, moving on to the next idea. Priya did not argue. She did not need to.

She just fixed the search function that week. Users noticed immediately, the way people notice when something quietly broken suddenly works. The notification system never got built. Nobody noticed that either.

That is what taste looks like in a meeting. Not more ideas than everyone else. Fewer, and the right one, chosen while the room was busy saying yes to all of them.
