---
title: "The Hidden Labour of AI"
date: 2026-02-01
pageDescription: "Behind every effortless AI result sits several rounds of correction nobody mentions afterward."
---
People talk about AI like it is a magic wand. You hear the same line everywhere: I told the AI and it did it. As if the work simply ended the moment the instruction went in.

That is not what actually happens. You describe what you want, and the AI hands back a version that is maybe seventy percent right. Close, but not quite there. So you correct it. You give more instructions. It hands back another version, better in one place and worse in another. You fix that too. Back and forth, three rounds, five rounds, sometimes ten, and somewhere in the middle you lose count entirely.

Then comes the part nobody mentions when they tell the story afterward. You stitch the pieces together. You check for mistakes. You test it properly. You fix the things it got wrong that you did not catch the first time around.

That is not nothing. That is work. A different kind of work than building it yourself from scratch, but work all the same, with its own particular kind of fatigue. The wand was never really magic. Someone still has to hold it steady, correction after correction, round after round.
