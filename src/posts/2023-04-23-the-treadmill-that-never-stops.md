---
title: "The Treadmill That Never Stops"
date: 2023-04-23
pageDescription: "Feeling great after a win fades fast because your expectations rise right along with it."
---
You get the promotion, the car, or the milestone you have been chasing, and for a while it feels as good as you imagined. Then something quiet happens. Your brain adjusts to the new normal, and you are back to wanting the next thing.

Researchers call this hedonic adaptation. Brickman and Campbell described the pattern in 1971, and in 1978 Brickman, Coates, and Janoff-Bulman tested it directly in a study of lottery winners. Even a jackpot did not produce lasting happiness. People adapted to their new circumstances and returned close to their old baseline.

This is the treadmill. It is not that you are ungrateful or hard to please. Your expectations rise to match whatever you achieve, so the feeling of enough keeps moving just out of reach, one step ahead of you, no matter how fast you walk.

The only way off is not a bigger win. It is recognizing that the treadmill was never going anywhere in the first place. There is no destination waiting at the far end of the belt, no finish line that finally satisfies. Once you see that, stepping off starts to look less like giving up and more like waking up.
