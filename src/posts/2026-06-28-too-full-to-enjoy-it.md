---
title: "Too Full to Enjoy It"
date: 2026-06-28
pageDescription: "Not burnout, not failure, just too full, and fullness turned out to be its own separate problem."
---
I noticed it on an ordinary evening. I was three tabs deep into a tutorial I had already forgotten the point of, and when I finally closed the laptop, I caught my own reflection in the dark window behind it.

I looked tired, but not the sleepy kind. The kind where your eyes are open and something behind them has gone quiet.

For a long time I could not name what I was feeling. It was not burnout. I was not failing at anything. I was not even unhappy, not exactly. I was just full. Too full. Full of options, full of projects, full of things I could be doing but was not, and things I was doing that I probably should not have been.

That distinction matters more than it sounds like it should. Overwork has an obvious fix, do less, rest more. Fullness does not work that way. You can be perfectly rested and still feel this way, because the problem is not your hours. It is the sheer number of directions pulling at your attention at once.

Naming it as fullness, not failure, was the first useful thing I did.
