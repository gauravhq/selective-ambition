---
title: "You Sound Like Your Father"
date: 2026-03-01
pageDescription: "A proud list of everything I was juggling sounded like an achievement until my mother heard it differently."
---
There was a stretch when I was genuinely proud of how busy I was. I would list my schedule out loud like it was an achievement, the day job and then the evening project and then the weekend thing I was trying to learn on top of it. People would say "wow, that's a lot," and I heard admiration in it, not concern.

My mother heard something else. I had her on speakerphone one evening, still typing while we talked, and she could hear it. She asked what I was doing. I listed five things without pausing.

She went quiet for a few seconds. Then she said, "You sound like your father before he got his blood pressure medication."

I laughed it off and told her I was fine. But I thought about that sentence for weeks afterward, the way a true thing sits differently in your chest than a false one does.

The admiration I had been collecting was the wrong kind, or at least an incomplete kind. It was the kind that feeds the performance instead of questioning it, and I had been mistaking it for something healthier.
