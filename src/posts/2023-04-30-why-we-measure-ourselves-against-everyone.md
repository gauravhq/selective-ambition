---
title: "Why We Measure Ourselves Against Everyone"
date: 2023-04-30
pageDescription: "Comparing yourself to others is not a character flaw but wiring built for a much smaller world."
---
You do not have an internal gauge that tells you how you are doing. No dial for smart enough, or successful enough, or on track. So you borrow one from the people around you. Smart enough? You check your peers. Successful enough? You check your colleagues. This is not a flaw. It is how you are wired.

In 1954, the psychologist Leon Festinger described this as social comparison theory, the idea that without an objective standard, people size themselves up against others nearby. Festinger was writing about villages, where the relevant group was maybe fifty people. Now the comparison group is the internet, billions of people, nearly all of them showing their best moment and hiding the rest. Same wiring. A completely different scale.

That gap is the trap. You are not losing to fifty neighbors anymore. You are losing to a curated highlight reel drawn from the whole planet, and no one can win that contest.

Knowing the comparing itself is hardwired, not a personal failing, takes some of the shame out of it. You are not weak for noticing who is ahead. You are just running old wiring on a new and much bigger village.
