---
title: "A Small Death of a Possible Self"
date: 2024-11-10
pageDescription: "Every idea you set down feels like losing a version of who you could be."
---
Saying no to your own idea is never just a decision about a project. It is a decision about who you are.

If your identity is built on possibility, if "I'm the person with twelve ideas and the energy to chase them all" is how you introduce yourself to yourself, then shelving one of those ideas is not neutral. It is saying no to the version of you who could have done it.

That is why it hurts more than the size of the decision seems to justify. Every shelved idea is a small death of a possible self, and your brain treats that loss the way it treats other real losses, even though nothing external has actually happened.

The way through is not to argue yourself out of the feeling. It is to build a different identity to stand in the old one's place: not "the person who could do everything," but "the person who chose what matters." That second identity is quieter. It will not impress anyone at a party the way a long list of projects does.

But it is the one that actually lets you finish something.
