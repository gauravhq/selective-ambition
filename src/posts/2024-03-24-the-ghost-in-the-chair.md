---
title: The Ghost in the Chair
date: 2024-03-24
pageDescription: "Doing everything right in the kitchen does not always mean your mind actually follows you."
---
Choosing the friction does not always work. I want to be honest about that part, because it would be easy to make this sound simpler than it actually is.

About two weeks into trying, I closed the laptop at the right time, walked to the kitchen, sat down, did everything that was supposed to matter. And my mind stayed at the desk anyway. My wife was talking about a hard conversation she had with a coworker, and I was nodding in what I hoped were the right places while quietly solving a bug in my head.

She stopped mid-sentence. "You're not here," she said. Not angry. Tired, which somehow felt worse. I apologized, but badly, the kind of apology where you say sorry while also feeling a little annoyed at having been caught not listening.

We sat in a silence that was not the good kind for a while after that.

Choosing the friction is not a switch you flip once and leave on. It is a practice, something you keep choosing over and over, and practices have bad days. That night was one of them, and pretending otherwise would not have helped either of us.
