---
title: Having It All Is a Myth
date: 2025-06-15
pageDescription: "The math behind having it all never actually adds up the way it claims."
---
Having it all sounds like a real, achievable thing. Like something you could reach if you planned well enough, woke up early enough, optimised hard enough for long enough. It gets talked about as a destination you can arrive at.

But the math does not work, and it never really has. Every time you add something to the pile, something else gets squeezed to make room for it. Usually sleep. Sometimes relationships. Often the quiet, unscheduled space where good ideas actually come from, the space that gets filled first because it looks empty on a calendar.

Nobody who appears to have it all actually has it all. Look closer and you find a person who does a few things well and a long list of other things they are quietly failing at, mostly out of view. The failures do not make it into the photo. Nobody posts the version where the laundry is three weeks behind and a friend has not heard from them in months.

Having it all was never a real place you could stand in. It was always a story about a place, told by people standing well outside your actual life.
