---
title: "Tiny but Doable"
date: 2024-08-04
pageDescription: "Picking one thing to work on means moving quickly on it rather than slowly and carefully."
---
Picking one thing does not mean moving slowly on it. That distinction took me longer to understand than it should have.

Andrew Ng has a phrase for the opposite approach that stuck with me: tiny but doable. The idea is that a prototype which barely works, shipped in a weekend, is worth more than a polished version you spent six months building for a problem nobody actually has. He also talks about a version of a weekend test: if you can build a rough version of something in two days, you probably should just build it, rather than spend those two days planning around it.

The answer you are looking for tends to be on the other side of actually doing the thing, not on the other side of thinking carefully about it beforehand.

I needed that permission more than I expected to. I had quietly confused "selective" with "slow," as if choosing one project instead of ten also meant treating that one project with extra caution, extra planning, extra weight.

It does not work that way. Picking one thing means you move quickly on it. Fewer projects, faster motion, not fewer projects and more hesitation.
