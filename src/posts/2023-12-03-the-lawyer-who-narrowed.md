---
title: The Lawyer Who Narrowed
date: 2023-12-03
pageDescription: "Choosing one specialty over many cost her clients at first, then made her irreplaceable."
---
Picture a lawyer who spent ten years trying to be good at everything. Corporate law, intellectual property, contracts, compliance, a bit of employment law on the side. Useful in a lot of rooms. The go-to person in none of them.

Then she picks one thing, intellectual property, and lets the rest go.

It costs her. She loses two major clients during the transition, both of whom wanted a generalist they could call for anything. The first year of narrowing is financially terrifying, full of months where she is not sure she made the right call.

But the specificity pays off. Three years later, firms are calling her specifically for patent disputes. Nobody else in her network handles that particular problem the way she does now.

The strange part is that getting narrower does not make her less valuable. It makes her the only call for a certain kind of problem, instead of one of several reasonable calls for many kinds.

And the part she notices most is smaller than any of that. When someone asks her something outside patents now, she can say, that is not my area. And it feels fine, not like a failure to be covered up.
