---
title: "A Person Working on One Thing"
date: 2024-08-18
pageDescription: "Saying you are working on one thing sounds smaller and somehow feels far more real."
---
Picking one project changed something about how I introduced myself, and at first that felt like a demotion.

The old version of me, asked what I was working on at a gathering, would list four things and watch eyebrows go up. It sounded impressive. It felt impressive, even though nothing on the list was actually finished.

The new version says "one thing," and then describes it. And the conversation that follows is different. People ask real follow-up questions, and I have real answers, because I can talk about the specific problem I am stuck on this week instead of a vague summary of four half-started ideas.

I sound, now, like someone who is making something, rather than someone who is thinking about making things. From the outside, that might read as smaller. Fewer projects, a shorter answer at gatherings, less to reel off when someone asks.

From the inside, it is more real, and the guilt that used to sit underneath every conversation about my work has mostly gone quiet. Carrying nine unfinished ideas in your head has a weight to it that you stop noticing until it is gone. One thing, it turns out, is light.
