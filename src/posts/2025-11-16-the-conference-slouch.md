---
title: "The Conference Slouch"
date: 2025-11-16
pageDescription: "Watching a peer succeed can rearrange how you see yourself in about four seconds."
---
I was at a small conference, the kind where everyone wears the same lanyard and pretends to enjoy the coffee. Near the back of a session, half listening, when a guy I vaguely knew took the stage to present his numbers.

The numbers were good. Better than anything I had built. And I felt it happen in my body before my mind caught up. My shoulders dropped first. I did not decide to slouch, it just happened. Then my jaw tightened. Then my brain started doing the arithmetic without asking: he started around when I started, he is there, I am here, therefore I am behind.

None of that was a conscious thought. Nobody sat down and reasoned it through. It just happened, the way water finds the lowest point without being asked to. Four seconds, maybe less, and my whole afternoon was gone, replayed instead of lived.

The strange part is that I liked the guy. I did not want to compete with him. I was not trying to turn him into a rival. But my body had already decided the two of us were in some kind of race, and it never once checked with me first.
