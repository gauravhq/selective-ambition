---
title: "The Structural No Required No Courage"
date: 2024-11-24
pageDescription: "Deleting one app on a Saturday did more for a life than a year of willpower."
---
I kept saying yes to after-hours Slack messages. Not because I wanted to answer them, but because I could not ignore the fact that someone was waiting on the other end.

So one Saturday, I deleted Slack from my phone. No announcement, no explanation posted anywhere. I just removed the app and went on with my weekend.

Monday came, and for the first time in months, my evenings belonged to me again. Nobody complained. A few people emailed instead, for things that were genuinely urgent, and that alone filtered out almost everything else that used to reach me after hours.

What struck me afterward was how little the whole thing had cost. The structural no did not require courage the way a flat refusal does. It required a five-second decision to delete an app.

And it did more for my time than a full year of trying, and mostly failing, to say no to individual requests one at a time. Some no's are not decisions you make in the moment. They are decisions you build into the shape of your day, once, so you never have to make them again.
