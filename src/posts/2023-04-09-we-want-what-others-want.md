---
title: "We Want What Others Want"
date: 2023-04-09
pageDescription: "Many of your ambitions may be borrowed from other people rather than chosen by you."
---
Not every want starts inside you. Some of it is borrowed, and mimetic desire is the name for how that happens.

The idea comes from Rene Girard, developed across several of his books, most accessibly in Deceit, Desire, and the Novel. His claim is simple and a little uncomfortable. We rarely decide to want something on our own. We want it partly because someone else already wants it. Desire is imitative, not invented from scratch. You want the promotion partly because your colleague wants it too. You want to start the side project partly because you watched someone else start theirs.

This is not a character flaw. It is how human desire actually works, and it explains something strange, why so many ambitions feel urgent but never quite personal. Urgency and ownership are not the same thing, and the two are easy to confuse when everyone around you seems to want the same thing at once.

I found this unsettling to sit with. Considering that most of what I had spent years chasing might not have been mine to begin with was not a comfortable evening.
