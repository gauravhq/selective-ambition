---
title: Grief in the Supermarket Queue
date: 2025-06-08
pageDescription: "Grieving a version of yourself that was always going to be impossible."
---
I was standing in a supermarket queue, holding a basket with nothing in it but milk and bread, scrolling through my project list on my phone the way you check a sore spot to see if it still hurts.

I noticed I could not picture finishing any of them. Not one. I could picture starting easily enough, the excitement of a first week always came easy. But finish. The word felt foreign, almost like a language I had stopped practising years ago.

Right there, between the bread aisle and the till, I felt something I had not expected. Grief. Real grief, not for something I had lost, but for something that had never actually existed in the first place. The version of me who could do all of it, finish all of it, hold all of it at once without dropping a single piece.

That person was not behind schedule somewhere. That person was never possible to begin with, not for me, not for anyone standing in that queue with their own basket and their own private list.

I had been measuring myself against an impossible man for years, and losing the comparison was never really a loss at all.
