---
title: "AI Doesn't Reduce Work, It Intensifies It"
date: 2023-03-05
pageDescription: "New AI tools often add supervising and correcting work instead of removing work entirely."
---
The pitch was simple. AI would take the busywork off your plate, and you would finally have room to breathe. For a lot of people, that is not what happened.

Multiple reports from Harvard Business Review, published between 2025 and 2026, documented something closer to the opposite. AI tools often do not reduce work, they intensify it. The pace gets faster and the scope gets wider, simply because both are suddenly possible. And a whole new layer of labor appears underneath the old one, supervising outputs, fact-checking claims, correcting mistakes, and integrating results across a growing pile of separate tools.

Workers describe brain fry, a creeping sense that their competence is under threat, plus constant pressure to keep adapting. None of that sounds like relief.

The promise was that these tools would free workers from the grinding parts of the job. For plenty of people, they simply handed over more to do, not less, and dressed it up as an upgrade.

I recognized brain fry in myself well before it had a name. Reading that someone had finally described the exact feeling was oddly steadying, even though the feeling itself had not gone anywhere.
