---
title: "70 Percent Right Is a Trap"
date: 2026-01-25
pageDescription: "An almost right result asks for a stranger kind of focus than starting over."
---
When something is completely wrong, it is almost a relief. You throw it away and start again, no attachment, no debate with yourself.

Seventy percent right is a different problem. It is close enough that throwing it away feels wasteful, so instead you fix it. You nudge one part, then another. And that nudging asks for a stranger, harder kind of concentration than building from nothing would. You are holding two things in your head at the same time, what you actually wanted and what you were handed. You compare them constantly, spot the gap, close it, then check that fixing one thing did not quietly break something that was already right.

It feels like editing someone else's rough draft. Except the someone else is a machine. It does not get tired the way a human collaborator would. It does not sense your frustration, does not slow down, does not pause to explain itself. It simply hands you the next version, confident as ever, and waits for you to find what is still wrong.

Seventy percent looks like almost there. It behaves like a trap. The last thirty percent is where most of the real work quietly lives.
