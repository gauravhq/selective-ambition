---
title: "The Coriander Moment"
date: 2024-09-08
pageDescription: "A two-day problem finally loosened while talking about groceries instead of staring at code."
---
Ten days into working on just one project, I had been stuck for two days on a problem in the data layer, staring at the same diagram without seeing what was wrong.

That evening my wife asked about weekend plans. We ended up talking about groceries for a couple of minutes, nothing important. And right in the middle of her saying something about running out of coriander, the answer showed up.

Not the whole answer. Just a thread. What if I flipped the order of two steps I had been treating as fixed. I had not considered it because for two days I had been approaching the problem from exactly the same direction, over and over.

I grabbed my laptop, changed four lines, and ran it. The shape of the thing was right for the first time.

That kind of moment does not happen when your attention is split across five projects at once. It happens when one problem has had enough uninterrupted time in your head that your mind can hand you an answer sideways, while you are thinking about coriander instead of code.
