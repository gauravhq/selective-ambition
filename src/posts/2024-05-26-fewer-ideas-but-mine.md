---
title: Fewer Ideas, but Mine
date: 2024-05-26
pageDescription: "Fewer inputs meant fewer ideas at first, then a smaller batch that actually held up."
---
After a few weeks of cutting my inputs, the volume of my ideas dropped. Sharply, and fast. For about two weeks I barely had a new idea at all, and that scared me more than I expected. It felt like I had broken something that used to just work on its own.

Then, around week three, a different kind of idea started showing up. Fewer of them, maybe two or three a week instead of the twenty I was used to. But they were mine. They came from problems I was actually facing that day, not from an echo of a podcast or someone else's tweet bouncing around in my head looking for a place to land.

They were smaller. Stranger. Less impressive sounding, if I am honest about how they read on the page. But they held up the next morning. The old reactive ideas, the ones I used to generate constantly, usually felt stale by Thursday, already replaced by whatever I had consumed since.

If FOMO is a fire, inputs are the fuel. Fewer inputs meant a smaller fire. It also meant the few flames left were actually mine.
