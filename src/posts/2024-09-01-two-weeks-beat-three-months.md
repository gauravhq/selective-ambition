---
title: "Two Weeks Beat Three Months"
date: 2024-09-01
pageDescription: "Two weeks of focus produced more progress than three months of trying to do everything."
---
Within two weeks of picking one project and setting the others down, I had made more progress than I had in the previous three months of trying to do everything at once.

That gap stopped me for a second when I noticed it. Two weeks against three months is not a small difference. It is the kind of gap that makes you question what you were actually doing with all that earlier time.

The project was not moving faster because I was suddenly working harder. I was not staying up later or skipping more weekends. The hours were roughly the same as before.

What had changed was attention. Before, those hours were split five or six ways, thin enough that nothing ever built on what came before it. Now the same hours were landing on the same problem, day after day, so each session picked up exactly where the last one left off instead of starting cold.

Same hours, more attention, and the difference in output was not incremental. It was the kind of difference that makes you wonder how much of the old busyness was ever really progress at all.
