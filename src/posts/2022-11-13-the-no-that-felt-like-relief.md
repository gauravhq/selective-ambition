---
title: The No That Felt Like Relief
date: 2022-11-13
pageDescription: "If a no left you feeling relieved, that relief was information all along."
---
Think of a time you said no and the very next feeling was relief. Not guilt, not the anxious replay of whether you handled it right, just a clean, immediate lightness, like setting down something heavy you had been holding for too long without noticing the weight.

That feeling is worth paying attention to, because it is information. Guilt after a no usually means you are still unsure whether it was the right call. Relief means something different. Relief tends to show up when the thing you turned down was never really yours to carry in the first place. It had landed on your plate somehow, through habit, or obligation, or simply because nobody else stepped up, and your no was really just you handing it back to where it belonged.

So think back to your own version of that no. What was it exactly. A request, a project, an invitation, a role you were expected to take on without ever being asked if you wanted it.

And notice the relief itself as a kind of answer. It is not a sign that you did something wrong by refusing. It is often the clearest sign you were right.
