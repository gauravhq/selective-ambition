---
title: "The Goalpost That Always Moves"
date: 2025-11-09
pageDescription: "Reaching a goal you worked toward for months can dissolve within days of arriving."
---
Here is how it usually goes. You set a goal, you work toward it, you achieve it, and for a day or two you feel great. Then you look around. She got promoted faster than you did. His product has more users than yours. The feeling of being ahead lasts about as long as it takes to notice someone else's version of the same win.

I know the shape of this one from the inside. Two years ago I set one clear goal: build and launch a complete product, not a prototype, something real people could use. It took eight months, two more than I had planned.

I launched it. People used it. By any honest measure, I had done what I set out to do. The accomplishment lasted roughly three days. By day six, I was sitting in front of a blank document, planning the next thing. The goal I had worked toward for eight months had dissolved the moment I reached it, replaced by a bigger one I never chose.

The goalposts do not just move. They accelerate. Most days, they are not sitting where you put them. They are sitting wherever the internet put them last.
