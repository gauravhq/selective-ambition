---
title: "Comparison Finds Every Profession"
date: 2025-10-26
pageDescription: "Comparison finds teachers and photographers as easily as it finds anyone working in tech."
---
This is not only a technology problem, even though technology is where the examples pile up easiest.

Think of a teacher who sees another teacher's classroom video go viral. It looks effortless. Thirty engaged kids, a lesson that flows, decorations that look like they belong in a magazine. She knows that there were probably a dozen takes behind that clip and a weekend of preparation before it. She knows this clearly, and she still feels inadequate walking into her own classroom the next morning.

Think of a photographer scrolling through other people's work late one evening. By the time she closes the app, her own website feels suddenly outdated, a little embarrassing. She opens her files to look at images she genuinely loved a week ago, and now they look flatter somehow, less interesting than she remembered. Nothing changed about the photographs themselves. Every image is exactly as it was. What changed is the eyes looking at them, recalibrated by an hour of scrolling through someone else's highlight reel.

Different professions, same mechanism. Comparison does not check your job title before it moves in. It just needs a feed, a screen, and a few unguarded minutes to work with.
