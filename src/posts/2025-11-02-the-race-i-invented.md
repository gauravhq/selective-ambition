---
title: "The Race I Invented"
date: 2025-11-02
pageDescription: "Counting your output against a stranger online is a race nobody else knows exists."
---
I caught myself doing something absurd a while back. I was counting. Literally counting how many projects I had built with AI tools, because someone online had mentioned building twelve in six months.

I had built four. And my brain, without asking my permission, decided that four was not enough. That I was behind. That a stranger I had never met, whose circumstances I knew nothing about, was winning some competition I had entered without noticing.

I was competing in a race I had invented myself, against a person who did not know they were in it, using rules I had made up on the spot, keeping score on a board nobody else could even see. And somehow, by those invented rules, I was losing.

If a friend described that exact situation to me, I would tell them, gently, that they had lost their mind. Feeling bad about a number you made up, based on one stranger's post, in a competition that does not exist anywhere outside your own head. But from inside it, none of that absurdity is visible. It does not feel crazy. It feels like motivation. It feels like ambition. It feels, convincingly, like having standards.
