---
title: "Five Questions, No Worksheet"
date: 2024-12-29
pageDescription: "Five questions replaced a system for deciding what belongs on your list and what does not."
---
I never built a system for deciding what stays on my list and what goes. What I have instead is five questions that kept showing up, over and over, whenever something needed sorting.

The first is the nobody-knew test. If nobody ever knew I did this thing, would I still want to do it? That strips out the performance and leaves what is actually mine.

The second is the borrowed-desires test. Whose voice is this, really, mine or someone I once wanted to impress?

The third is the not-now shelf. Not never, just not this season. Putting something down is not the same as throwing it away.

The fourth is the one-thing-season question. If this year could move only one thing forward, which one would it be? Most of the time I already know.

The fifth is the tiny-but-doable rule. Pick something small enough to finish and specific enough that you will know when you have.

That is the whole list. No scoring sheet, no worksheet at the back of a book. Oddly, they seem to work better the less hard you try to use them.
