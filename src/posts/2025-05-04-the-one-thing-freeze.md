---
title: "The One-Thing Freeze"
date: 2025-05-04
pageDescription: "Before you can answer what you truly want, notice the freeze that hits when you try."
---
Try this. If you could only work on one thing for the next six months, what would it be?

Not three things. Not "a couple." One.

Notice what happens when you try to answer. For most people it is not a blank. It is a freeze, a small traffic jam behind the eyes. That freeze is not because you lack an answer. It is because you have too many, and every one of them has a decent case for why it deserves the slot.

Picking one means putting the others down, and putting them down feels less like prioritizing and more like failing them. Like admitting you cannot do it all, which is a hard thing to admit when doing it all has felt, for years, like the whole point. Closing one door always looks like giving up, even when it is really just choosing.

That freeze, the stall before you can even write a sentence, is not a problem to get past quickly on the way to the real answer. It is the real answer, or at least the start of it. Sit in it a while before you reach for relief.
