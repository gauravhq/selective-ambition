---
title: "Burnout Is a Loss of Agency"
date: 2023-02-12
pageDescription: "Burnout often comes less from long hours and more from losing any sense of choice."
---
Burnout is not simply a matter of too many hours on the clock. Drawing on the work of Christina Maslach and Michael Leiter, researchers describe it as having three parts, emotional exhaustion, depersonalization, meaning a growing detachment from the work itself, and a shrinking sense of accomplishment.

Here is the part that surprises people. You can burn out while doing work you genuinely love, if you lose the sense that you are the one choosing it. Plenty of meaningful jobs still burn people out, and plenty of demanding jobs never do, which suggests hours were never the real variable.

The key factor is not volume. It is agency. When work happens to you, arriving as obligation after obligation with no sense of a choice behind it, burnout follows regardless of how meaningful that work actually is.

This is part of why JOMO, which is fundamentally about choosing what you do and do not carry, may be one of the better antidotes available. Not less work in every case. More work that is actually chosen, which changes how the exact same hours feel to carry.
