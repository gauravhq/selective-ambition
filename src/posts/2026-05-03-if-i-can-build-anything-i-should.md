---
title: "If I Can Build Anything, I Should Build Everything"
date: 2026-05-03
pageDescription: "Having the ability to build everything is not the same as being obligated to build it."
---
Here is the part I did not want to admit for a long time. The problem was never that I had too much to do. The problem was that I believed I should do all of it.

The wall coming down did not just hand me the ability to build things. It handed me a new kind of pressure, one I had not felt before, because it had never been relevant before. If I can build anything now, the thinking went, then I should be building everything. And if I am not building everything, I am wasting this moment. I am wasting the opportunity that just opened up in front of me. I am falling behind people who are moving faster than I am.

That thought sounds almost reasonable when you are inside it. It does not feel like pressure. It feels like ambition, like drive, like finally taking your own ideas seriously after years of just writing them down.

But it is the real trap. Not the number of ideas on the list. The quiet belief that having the ability to do something means you are obligated to do it.
