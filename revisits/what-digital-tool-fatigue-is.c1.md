When I finally counted my own tools, the professional ones alone came to eight. Eight platforms I checked at least once a day, each with its own badge count and its own little red dot saying look at me. I was spending more time moving between contexts than I spent in any single one. The actual work kept getting pushed to the margins.

Separately, one small move did a lot for my evenings. I had been saying yes to too many after-hours Slack messages, so I deleted Slack from my phone on a Saturday morning, without announcing it. It took about five seconds. On Monday my evenings belonged to me for the first time in months. A few people started emailing me for urgent things instead, which meant only the truly urgent reached me.

I didn't need to see everything. I needed to see the right things. [The Structural No](/blog/the-structural-no/) is the name for that kind of move.
