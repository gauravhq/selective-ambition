A yes is hard to price because only one side of the trade has a name. The committee has a name. The two evenings a week it takes from you do not.

Think of someone in HR at a local government office who joins every committee, sure that visibility is the path to promotion. Two years later, no promotion. In a meeting about a policy document nobody will read, her phone buzzes with a photo of her son on stage, in the costume she helped him make. She isn't there.

That photo is the price, finally with a name on it. She drops one committee, then another, keeping only the one tied to her work and the one she cares about. Three months later she is passed over for a project lead role and can't tell whether the two are connected. The following month, she is at the school play. [Every Yes Is a Hidden No](/blog/every-yes-is-a-hidden-no/).
