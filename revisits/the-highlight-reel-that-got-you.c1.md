The one that got me wasn't even on a screen. It was at a small conference. A man I vaguely knew from online, who had started in the same space around the same time as me, took the stage with his product and his numbers. Annoyingly good numbers. My shoulders dropped before I had decided anything. Then my jaw tightened. Then my brain started the math: he started when I started, so I must be behind.

I liked the guy. I didn't want to feel small. But comparison had taken two data points, stripped away every bit of context, and delivered a verdict. I didn't hear another word of that session.

Four seconds is all it takes. With another comparison I caught in myself, counting my own projects against a stranger's total, what worked was slow: noticing the reflex, interrupting it, losing the thread, coming back. Slowly the urge got quieter. [The Conference Slouch](/blog/the-conference-slouch/).
