Saying no Draft

The Three Types of No

By Gaurav Srivastava

Not every no is the same, and knowing which one you are saying makes it easier.

Saying no feels like a single, dreaded act. It is actually three different tools, and confusing them is part of why it is so hard.

The hard no is a clean refusal: no, and no explanation owed. Use it for the things that are simply not yours to carry.

The soft no buys room without burning the bridge: not now, not this season, ask me again in spring. Use it when the answer is really about timing, not the thing itself.

The structural no removes the question before it is asked: no meetings before noon, no new projects this quarter, no phone at dinner. You decide once, and the decision does the saying for you, so you are not spending willpower on the same battle every day.

Most of us default to a mumbled, guilty version of the soft no for everything, which pleases no one. Pick the right instrument. Every yes you actually mean gets paid for by a hundred quiet nos, so choose those nos deliberately.

This is one idea from Selective Ambition: The Joy of Missing Out on Purpose by Gaurav Srivastava — the case for caring deeply about fewer things, and letting the rest go on purpose.
