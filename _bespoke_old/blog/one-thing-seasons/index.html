One-Thing Seasons

By Gaurav Srivastava

You do not need a five-year plan. You need a this-season answer to one question.

When you carry ten projects, "the person with a million ideas" becomes part of who you are. Creative. Ambitious. Always building something. Setting nine of them down does not just free your calendar; it threatens your sense of self.

That is why focus is so hard. It is not a scheduling problem, it is an identity one.

The way through is to think in seasons instead of forever. You are not killing the other ideas. You are putting them on a shelf, not in the bin. They will keep. This season, one thing gets your depth; the rest wait, and waiting does them no harm.

So the question is not "what is my five-year plan?" It is smaller and far more useful: what is the one thing this season? Everything else can genuinely wait.

Depth is the reward almost no one collects, because it only shows up after you stop starting new things long enough for the work to get interesting. A season is long enough. Pick one.

This is one idea from Selective Ambition: The Joy of Missing Out on Purpose by Gaurav Srivastava — the case for caring deeply about fewer things, and letting the rest go on purpose.
